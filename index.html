How it works

Pair once. Then just copy.

Sync is off until you switch it on, and no network port is opened before then. Pairing takes four steps.

OFFICE-PC
Sync over Local Network

Switch on to find your other devices on this network.

Sync over Local Network

Switch on to find your other devices on this network.

Both devices on the same local network

From then on, what you copy on either one is put on the other’s clipboard and added to its list. Pair a third device with any member of the group and it syncs with all of them.

Things to know

  • The first time sync is switched on, Windows asks whether to let the app through the firewall and macOS asks for access to the local network. Sync needs both to be allowed.
  • Networks that keep devices apart, such as guest Wi-Fi and some office networks, will not work.
  • Text and pictures are synced. Pictures larger than 5 MB as PNG stay on the device where they were copied.
  • Files are never sent: a path means nothing on another machine. A device that is off or away when something is deleted does not hear about it.

Features

Everything you copy, where you need it.

A history you can search, sync between your own devices, and an app that stays out of the way until you reach for it.

Clipboard history

Everything you copy, newest first.

PinnedTodayYesterdayThis WeekEarlier
  • Records text, pictures (screenshots, images copied from a page or an editor) and files copied in a file manager, newest first.
  • Groups entries under Pinned, Today, Yesterday, This Week and Earlier.
  • Preview an entry in full, pin it, or delete it.
  • A deleted entry can be brought back for a few seconds.
  • Copying the same thing again moves it to the top instead of adding a duplicate.

Clipboard sync

Your devices become one hive.

  • Copy on one device, paste on another: text and pictures.
  • More than two devices can share one group: pair a new device with any member and it syncs with all of them.
  • Deleting an entry, undoing the delete and Clear History carry over to every connected device.
  • Optionally share the text already in your history with a newly connected device.

Privacy and security

Your clipboard stays on your network.

What PasteHive does to protect it, and, just as plainly, what it does not do.

By design

No cloud, no account, no telemetry.

Your clipboard goes only to devices you have accepted, directly over your own network. There is no PasteHive server.

By design

Encrypted in transit.

Each connection does a fresh X25519 key exchange, derives its keys from that and the group’s key together (HKDF-SHA256), and encrypts every message with ChaCha20-Poly1305. A device without the group’s key can neither join nor read.

Know this

Visible on your network while sync is on.

Devices announce themselves by name over UDP (port 45817, multicast group 239.255.77.67 and broadcast) so that others on the same network can find them. What you copy is sent only to devices you have accepted.

Know this

Stored on your device, unencrypted.

History is kept in history.json in the app’s support folder as plain text, copied pictures as PNG files in an images folder beside it (at most 40 unpinned ones), and the group’s key in sync.json. Anyone with access to your user account can read them.

Know this

Not independently reviewed.

The protocol is assembled from standard parts but was written for this app and has not had a security audit. Do not rely on it for secrets you could not afford to leak.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

  • Yes. It is open source under the MIT license.

  • No. Devices connect to each other directly on the local network. There is no PasteHive server.

  • No. Both devices have to be on the same local network.

  • Yes, for text and pictures, once the devices are paired. On Android, pictures arrive in the list but cannot yet be put on the clipboard.

  • It works alongside them. PasteHive adds search, filters, pinning and sync between Windows, macOS and Android, which those do not cover together.

  • On a Windows laptop the idle app measured about 1.8% of one CPU core. On a phone, keeping sync on in the background costs more; switch sync off when you do not need it.

  • It records whatever is copied. Use Pause Capturing before copying something you do not want kept, or delete the entry afterwards.
